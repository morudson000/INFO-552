Colorblind Color Theory

Here we have an introduction to color theory as it relates to colorblind folks.
Geared towards those who have little to no experience with color theory, this page will help you understand how to use color in a way that is accessible to all.

1. What Is Colorblindness?

Colorblindness affects how people perceive certain colors. Common types include:

  • Deuteranopia: difficulty seeing green
  • Protanopia: difficulty seeing red
  • Tritanopia: difficulty seeing blue

Color vision impairment affects around 8 percent of men! This means that using colorblind friendly colors increases accessibility. Further, a study of 580 papers in biological sciences found that roughly half had figures that were completely or partially inacessable for red-green colorblind readers. Read more here.

2. Color Combinations to Avoid

These combinations often cause confusion for colorblind users:

  • Red & Green
  • Blue & Purple
  • Green & Brown

3. Colorblind-Safe Palettes

Multple color pallets have been developed by various artists and scientists and have been included here. These palettes are designed to be accessible for colorblind users while still being visually appealing. Of note is Paul Tol's work, which has been widely adopted in scientific publications. For more information regarding Paul's work and colorblindness, you can access his website here. Masataka Okabe and Kei Ito published a paper on colorblind-safe palettes in 2002, which has been widely used in scientific publications.

4. Best Practices

  • Use high contrast between text and background
  • Avoid using color alone to convey meaning
  • Label charts and include text alternatives
  • Use tools to simulate colorblindness
